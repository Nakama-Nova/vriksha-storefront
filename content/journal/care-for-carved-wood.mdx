A carved piece does not need much, but it does need the right kind of attention. Wood is a natural material: it takes in and gives off moisture, and it reacts to sun, water and cleaning products. A few habits will protect it.

## Dust gently, and often

Dust settles in the grooves of a carving and, over time, dulls the detail. Use a soft dry brush (a clean paintbrush or a soft makeup brush works well) to lift it out of the deep cuts, then wipe flat surfaces with a dry cotton cloth.

Skip damp rags on carved areas. Water gets into fine cuts and is slow to leave.

## Keep it out of direct sun and away from heat

Strong sunlight fades colour and can dry the wood unevenly, which is how hairline cracks start. Do not place a piece against a radiator or directly under an air-conditioner vent. A spot with steady, indirect light is best.

## Avoid sudden changes in humidity

Wood swells a little in damp weather and shrinks in dry weather. This is normal. What causes trouble is a fast swing, for example moving a piece from a humid room into a cold air-conditioned one. Let it settle in its new place.

## Polish sparingly

A thin coat of a good furniture wax or oil once or twice a year is plenty. More than that builds up in the carving and turns sticky. Always test on a hidden spot first, and rub the wax in with a soft cloth rather than leaving it on top.

## Do not use harsh cleaners

Avoid sprays that contain alcohol, bleach or strong solvents. They can strip the finish and leave pale patches. For a mark that will not wipe off, ask us before trying anything stronger.

## When to ask for help

If you notice a new crack, a loose joint or small holes with fine powder beneath them, please get in touch early. These are much easier to fix when small.
