The wood you choose decides how a piece looks, how it ages and how much care it needs. Here is a plain guide to some woods commonly used for furniture and carving in India.

## Teak

Teak is dense and naturally rich in oils, which helps it resist moisture and insects. It darkens gently with age to a deep golden brown. It is a good choice for pieces that will see daily use, such as beds, dining tables and doors.

## Rosewood (sheesham)

Rosewood is hard and heavy, with strong grain that polishes to a deep, handsome finish. It suits statement furniture and detailed carving, where the grain is part of the beauty.

## Mango wood

Mango wood is lighter in colour and more affordable, with a varied, lively grain. It takes paint and stain well, which makes it a practical option for painted pieces and lighter furniture.

## Sandalwood

Sandalwood is prized for its fine, even grain and its scent, and it is used mostly for small, finely detailed carvings rather than furniture.

## How to decide

- **Daily use or heavy loads:** choose a hard, dense wood.
- **Fine carving:** choose a wood with a tight, even grain that holds detail.
- **Painted finish:** a lighter wood with a plain grain is a good canvas.
- **Budget:** let us know, and we will suggest the best wood within it.

Every piece of wood is different, so grain and colour vary from one piece to the next. That is part of what makes a handmade piece yours.
